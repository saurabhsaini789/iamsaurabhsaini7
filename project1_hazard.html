Project 1

Title Hazard Identification, Assessment, and Control
Role Instructional Designer, Developer, and Author
Timeline Q4 2023, 6 Week Desing & Development
Client DSG & TDG
Team Size Sole Designer & Developer
Modality Self-Paced eLearning (SCROM)

Project Overview

Overview Designed and developed a comprehensive, 8-lesson eLearning course to empower employees with the knowledge and skills to proactively identify, assess, and control workplace hazards. The course translates complex occupational health and safety legislation into practical, actionable steps, fostering a proactive safety culture and reducing the risk of workplace incidents.
Problem / Opportunity / Need The organization needed to standardize safety training across all provinces, ensuring compliance with varying legislative requirements. There was a need to move beyond a compliance-checkbox mentality to an engaged, practical understanding of hazard management, reducing both acute injuries and long-term ergonomic issues.
Target Audience All employees, from warehouse and factory floor workers to delivery specialists and repair technicians. The audience had varied levels of formal education and hands-on experience, requiring content that was accessible, relatable, and directly applicable to their daily tasks.
Scope / Boundaries The scope included the full analysis, design, development, and implementation of the course. Boundaries were set to focus on the four primary hazard types (Physical, Chemical, Biological, Ergonomic) and the standardized 3-step process (Identify, Assess, Control), without delving into highly role-specific machinery training.

Goals & Objectives

Learning Objectives

Explain the legal requirements for hazard management.

Identify the four main types of workplace hazards (Physical, Chemical, Biological, Ergonomic).

Apply a logical 3-step process (Identify, Assess, Control) to manage hazards.

Assess risk using a defined matrix based on Probability, Severity, and Frequency.

Recommend appropriate control measures using the Hierarchy of Controls (Engineering, Administrative, PPE).

Differentiate between a Formal Hazard Assessment and a Field Level Hazard Assessment (FLHA).

Business Goals / KPIs

Achieve 100% compliance with provincial OH&S training mandates.

Reduce reportable workplace incidents and near-misses by 15% within one year.

Decrease lost-time injury rates, particularly those related to musculoskeletal disorders.

Standardize the hazard assessment process across all departments and job sites.

Tools & Technologies

Tools Used (Universal) Articulate Storyline Adobe Acrobat Pro Microsoft PowerPoint Adobe Creative Cloud
Technologies / Frameworks Adult Learning Theory (Andragogy) Bloom's Taxonomy SCCROM 1.2

Research & Discovery

Needs Analysis Conducted a gap analysis by reviewing previous incident reports and existing training materials. Discovered a primary need for practical application over theoretical knowledge, with a specific emphasis on risk assessment and the difference between acute and chronic hazards.
Baseline Metrics Analyzed past safety audit results and incident data, which showed a higher-than-average rate of strains and sprains, indicating a need for stronger ergonomic and risk assessment training.

Planning & Strategy

Methodology Applied an Agile approach with iterative storyboard development, allowing for rapid prototyping and feedback from key stakeholders in the Safety department.
Process Mapping MMapped the existing, informal hazard identification process versus the new, standardized 3-step process to visualize the improvement for stakeholders. .
Creative Brief Established a visual style that was professional, clean, and high-contrast for readability. Used a consistent color scheme and iconography to represent different hazard types and key concepts. .
Storyboard Created a detailed text-based storyboard outlining all on-screen content, narration script, interactive elements (flip cards, tabs), and knowledge check questions. .

Development & Process

Prototypes Built interactive prototypes in Articulate Storyline for the first two lessons (focusing on the flip-card interactions and hazard categorization tabs) to validate the user experience and instructional approach before full development..
Development Process Developed the full course in Articulate Storyline 360, utilizing built-in quizzing features for the final knowledge check and ensuring SCORM compatibility for the LMS.
Iterations & Feedback Cycles Presented the prototype and two subsequent lesson drafts to the Safety Manager. Incorporated feedback on terminology accuracy (e.g., "probable" vs. "likely" in the risk matrix) and the clarity of real-world examples (e.g., furniture assembly and delivery scenarios).

Deliverables

Final Learning Solution A fully functional, SCORM 1.2-compliant eLearning module, hosted on the company LMS.
Process Improvement Plan Standardized the 4-Phase Departmental Hazard Identification process and the Field Level Hazard Assessment (FLHA) procedure.
Design Assets Custom-designed icons for hazard types, interactive elements (flip cards, tabs), and a visually engaging risk matrix.
Course Materials A downloadable Job Aid summarizing the 3-step process and the Hierarchy of Controls for quick reference on the job.
Project Documentation Complete storyboard, design rationale document, and SCORM deployment guide.

Outcomes & Impact

Performance Improvement Metrics Post-course assessment scores averaged 94%, significantly exceeding the 80% pass threshold. The practical application of FLHAs by field teams increased by 40% in the first quarter post-launch.
User Engagement Metrics The course received positive informal feedback for its relatable examples and interactive format, with many users specifically mentioning the risk assessment examples as highly useful.
Time Saved Standardized the training process, reducing the time managers spent creating and delivering ad-hoc safety talks by an estimated 10 hours per month.
Client / Stakeholder Feedback The Safety Department Lead reported: "This course has given us a common language and framework for discussing safety. We're now having more proactive conversations about risk instead of reactive ones about blame."

Reflection

Key Learnings This project reinforced the power of structuring content around a simple, repeatable framework (the 3-step process). By anchoring complex concepts to a memorable core structure, retention and application were greatly improved. I also learned the critical importance of using the client's own terminology and real-world scenarios to build credibility and relevancet.
Challenges & How I Solved Them The biggest challenge was making the risk assessment matrix feel practical and not overly academic. I solved this by building a progressive interactive example where learners assign values to real hazards from the course (air compressor, furniture moving) and see the risk score calculated in real-time, directly linking theory to outcome.
What I’d Improve Next Time Given more time and resources, I would develop a series of short, 2-minute video testimonials from veteran employees sharing a "close call" story and how the hazard identification process could have prevented it. This would leverage social proof and make the content even more impactful.
